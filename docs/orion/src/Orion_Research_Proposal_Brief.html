Research Proposal Brief · 2 pages

Graph-of-Loops Control for Continuous Autonomous Business Operation

An extension of an already-evaluated department architecture. We ask whether mutually-checking control loops, anchored in code-computed measurement, can sustain a business operated by that architecture without proxy capture.
Vinayak Maurya · Department of Computer Engineering · University of Mumbai · 27 July 2026 · Full proposal: 14 pp

Central question

Can a graph of mutually-checking control loops, anchored in code-computed measurement, hard constraints and human judgement, sustain the continuous autonomous operation of a business without proxy capture — under conditions where a single goal-measure-act loop per department does not?

We do not expect a positive result to show the elimination of proxy capture. The more plausible outcome, if the hypotheses hold at all, is that capture becomes detectable sooner and to a measurable degree.

What is already established

RQ QuestionStatus Position
RQ1Multi-agent vs single-LLM baseline Partly measured Completeness favours decomposition decisively; consistency tied; financial accuracy favours the baseline
RQ2Dynamic routing efficiency Supported 17.3% token saving, 0.0 pp completeness delta
RQ3Grounding vs parametric knowledge Not measurable Requires competitor ground truth not yet collected

Two methodological findings are the load-bearing inputs to this proposal. M1: cross-agent constraints stated as prompt instructions with explicit numeric bounds were ignored repeatedly, and held only once re-implemented as post-conditions in code. M2: variance compounds along a dependency chain — over five runs of an identical prompt the pipeline's standard deviation was 103.8 against the baseline's 21.2, with the pipeline's best run more accurate than the baseline's best.

Both findings indicate that an agent cannot be relied upon to assert a quantity by which it is judged. We propose to generalise the local remedy — derived figures override asserted ones — into a control architecture for continuous operation.

Proposed questions and hypotheses

RQ4Does variance compounding persist when the estimated quantity is replaced by one computed from a record of events?
RQ5Does proxy-anchor divergence arise systematically under single-loop control, and how quickly?
RQ6Do independent monitor loops detect divergence earlier than a composite health indicator?
RQ7Are the graph invariants — cycle termination at an anchor or human, monitor independence — necessary for detection, or merely sufficient?

Six hypotheses are stated in the full proposal, each paired with an explicit falsification condition recorded before the arms are run. Two examples:

H1A margin computed from a record of transactions will show materially lower run-to-run variance than the same margin estimated through the agent chain.
Refuted if: the computed quantity's standard deviation is not materially below the 103.8 already measured.
H3Under the full loop-graph topology, terminal proxy-anchor divergence will be materially smaller than under single-loop control.
Refuted if: terminal divergence is statistically indistinguishable from the single-loop arm, or larger.

Method

The existing four-arm harness is extended, not replaced. The current arms — multi_agent, baseline, all_departments, no_grounding — remain unmodified, so results already obtained stay valid. Four arms are added: single_loop, anchors_only, monitors_only, full_graph. Metrics F54–F58 keep their definitions; F59–F63 are proposed: proxy-anchor divergence, detection latency, conflict incident rate, objective staleness and human decision load.

Every proposed metric is computed by the harness from the append-only record. None derives from any agent's account of its own performance — if the instrument for detecting proxy capture could itself be influenced by the departments under study, the study would exhibit the failure it proposes to measure.

Baseline fairness is the principal methodological risk. The single-loop arm receives the same memory, planning and execution machinery as the full-graph arm, differing only in control topology; its proxy metrics are drawn from measures actually used in comparable business functions rather than chosen for exploitability; and its specification is fixed and documented before any arm runs. We would rather report a null result against a strong baseline than a positive one against a weak baseline.

The existing work sets the precedent: its central finding is a negative one, reported rather than tuned away.

Principal threats to validity

  • Construct. Divergence may conflate Goodhart effects with ordinary action-to-effect lag, anchor mis-specification, or noise — and M2 shows noise here is substantial. The most serious threat, and not fully resolvable.
  • Internal. Non-determinism may explain between-arm differences; mitigated by paired seeds and distributional reporting. The full topology also performs more inference, so a compute-matched control is needed.
  • External. The operating environment is simulated; a churn decision is a modelling assumption, not an observation. The prompt set is India-specific.
  • Reflexivity. The same author designs the architecture, selects the anchors, defines divergence and interprets results. Pre-registration and artefact release are partial mitigations; independent replication would be stronger.

Prerequisites before the proposed arms can be interpreted: the full thirty-five-prompt sweep, sourced sector benchmarks, and collected competitor ground truth. Until then F54 remains provisional and F55 unmeasurable, and any operating-layer result resting on them inherits that status. Full proposal: 14 pp, 6 figures, 16 references.
